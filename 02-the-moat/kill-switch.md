## Portability Score
**Partial.** The parts that make the product valuable are ours: the record, the selection of findings, the calibration on confirmed outcomes and the certification file. But a certified medical device cannot switch to any model in 48 hours: only to a standby that has already passed validation and is described in the technical documentation. Today there is no standby, no gateway and no automated eval, and model and cloud sit with one company, so a switch would be blind and not allowed. With pinned versions, a validated open-weight standby that can also run on-premise, and the automated golden suite, the score moves to **Ready**, with the honest limit that "ready" means ready to switch to the standby, not to any new model.

## If Google doubles pricing tomorrow:
The impact on the margin is small: inference is about €91 of €1,661 per hospital per month (Module 3), so doubling it moves the gross margin from about 67% to about 65%. A price increase on Vertex AI hosting would weigh more, because infrastructure (€400) is a larger line than inference.

- **Hour 0–4:** Raise the share of updates that stay on the Flash-class model, and reuse the last suggestions until the selected findings change (the routing rule above).
- **Hour 4–24:** Check whether the validated standby still passes the golden suite on the current release, both on Vertex AI and on-premise.
- **Hour 24–48:** If the standby passes and is in the technical documentation, plan the switch for the next release, on-premise if hosting costs are the problem. If not, stay on Gemini and accept the price. Switching to a cheaper model that has not been validated is not an option for a certified device, whatever it saves.

## If Google ships a competing product:
Google already builds medical models and healthcare products on Google Cloud, so a clinical assistant that suggests diagnoses is a realistic move. What it cannot replicate:

- **The record and its updates:** the full history of each patient in our EHR, letters and reports from other hospitals through the eHealth exchange, and every new result as it arrives. Google sees only what we send for one call, and the data processing terms forbid using it for training.
- **Calibration on confirmed outcomes:** likelihood labels and severity percentages measured against the coded discharge diagnoses and severity of illness, per hospital. That is our data and our work, not the model's.
- **The certified status:** the clinical evaluation, quality system and post-market surveillance belong to our product. A provider that wants the same intended purpose in a European hospital has to build its own.
- **The place in the workflow and the hospital agreements:** the module sits where physicians already work, and the agreements on using outcome data are with us.

Because Google is both our model provider and our cloud provider, this scenario is the reason the standby must be able to run on-premise: the gateway and the open-weight standby let us leave Google for both, not just for the model.
